Where should San Ramon
plant its next trees?

Across San Ramon, tree canopy runs from under 6% to over 40% of the ground, and surface heat tracks it almost one-for-one — r = −0.80. What it does not track is income. So this is not a map of who is rich and who is poor. It is a map of where the shade is missing, what that costs in degrees, and what it would take to fix.

What should count most pipeline defaults

Each input is scaled 0–1 within this city, multiplied by its weight, and the results are added. That sum is then scaled by how many people live in the hex. Weights are relative: they are normalised to 100%, so raising one lowers the rest — which is why moving a single slider changes less than you expect, and why the presets move all of them at once. The first four defaults come from pipeline/config.py. Walk to relief is a fifth input the shipped pipeline does not use, so it starts at 0; raise it to weigh distance to a cooling site alongside the rest.
